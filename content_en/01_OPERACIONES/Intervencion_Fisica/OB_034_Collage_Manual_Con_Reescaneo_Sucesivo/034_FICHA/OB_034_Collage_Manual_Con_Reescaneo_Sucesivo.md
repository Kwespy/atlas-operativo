# OB_034 — Manual collage with successive rescanning

## Status
Status:: [[En revisión|Under review]]

## 1. Operation data

**Internal number:** OB_034  
**Operational regime:** [[Lista_Intervención_Física|Physical Intervention List]]  
**Source image:** [[ATLAS_OPERATIVO/04_INPUTS/Input_005.jpeg|Input 005.jpeg]] — frontal portrait printed on paper.

**Operational description:**  
Parts of a portrait print are cut out and glued onto another print of the same image. The collage is scanned; afterwards, new cutouts can be added and it can be scanned again. A scan of the collage can also be printed, cut out, and glued once more onto the composition. Thus, each return from the scanner turns a material paper arrangement into a new surface available for cutting and overlapping.

The selection and placement of each fragment are manual decisions. They are not determined by a cutting, pasting, and shifting algorithm: they are made by observing the relationships of light, contrast, form, and position that emerge in the image.

---

## 2. Tools and procedure

**Tools / medium:**
- Paper prints of the portrait and, when reused, of its scans.
- Cutting tool and adhesive to assemble the fragments.
- Scanner to register and reintroduce the states of the collage.

**Technical procedure:**  
1. Print the source portrait.
2. Cut fragments from the print and glue them onto another copy, deciding their position based on the visual relationships they produce.
3. Scan the intervened state.
4. Continue gluing fragments and scanning the successive states.
5. When desired, print a scan of the collage and use parts of that new print as material for another layer.

The received folder contains nine scans, dated between October 2 and October 5, 2026. The process remains open: new states can be incorporated. The filenames allow the records to be ordered, but do not document by themselves which print each cutout comes from or how many times a scan was reprinted.

**Script:** Not applicable. The operation is manual; the scanner records and returns material to the process.

**Step-by-step record:** the original name and the date inferred from that name are contained in the EXIF metadata (*ImageDescription*) of each cataloged copy. The pixels are identical to the desktop files.

---

## 3. Variables

**Controlled variables:**
- Which fragments of the portrait or a previous scan are cut.
- Scale, orientation, position, and overlap of the cutouts.
- Light and contrast relationships considered when deciding where to glue them.
- The moment when a printed image is scanned and reused.

**Uncontrolled / accidental variables:**
- Edges, small misalignments, and shadows produced by the overlapping paper.
- Tonal and chromatic changes introduced by printing and scanning.
- Exact provenance of each fragment in the nine current records: it is not documented.

## 5. Visual selection

### Crisis / threshold according to AI-assisted reading

In the records following the reference scan, the eyes, hair, and mouth remain recognizable, but their duplications and the paper edges prevent the portrait from being read as a single continuous face. The dark and light layers redistribute attention between features and background. This is a provisional reading of the sequence, not the choice of a crisis image.

## 6. Crisis and formal-perceptual reading

**The operation affects form, contour, light, figure/ground relationship, and recognition:**  
The face remains as an anchor of recognition, while the cutouts introduce other scales and positions of the same face over it. An eye can continue a gaze or interrupt it; a dark area of hair can become a compositional mass; the whiteness of the paper can open a gap between planes. The physical edges and tone differences between prints and scans make the construction of the collage visible.

The rescanning provisionally fixes a material layer and allows that layer to become printed matter once again. The image is not altered through calculated shifts, but through successive decisions made in front of what is seen. It has not yet been determined which of the states reaches the sought crisis threshold.

---

## 7. Final evaluation and artistic reference

**Operation notes / what I learned:**  
Kurt notes that this manual way of working feels more comfortable to him and offers more pictorial options than an algorithm that cuts, pastes, and changes the position of objects. He can decide where to place each fragment by attending to the contrasts and light of the ongoing composition. Furthermore, he finds the process more fun. This capacity for visual response during the making is a central part of the operation, not a subsequent adjustment to the result.
