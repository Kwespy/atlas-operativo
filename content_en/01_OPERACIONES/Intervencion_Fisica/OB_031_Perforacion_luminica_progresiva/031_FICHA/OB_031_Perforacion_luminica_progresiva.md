# OB_031 — Progressive light perforation

## Status
Status:: [[Terminada|Finished]]

---

## 1. Operation data

**Internal number:** OB_031  
**Operational regime:** [[Lista_Intervención_Física|Physical Intervention List]]  
**Source image:** [[Input_001.jpg.webp|Input 001.jpg.webp]] — technical image of a ball.

**Operational description:**  
The technical image is printed on paper and progressively perforated with a pointed pencil. The print is placed in front of a computer screen with a white background and photographed in each state: the light passes through the perforations and transforms the continuity of the image into a grid of dots, apertures, and areas of transparency.

---

## 2. Tools and procedure

**Tools / medium:**
- Paper print of the technical image
- Pointed pencil
- Computer screen with a white background
- Camera for documentation

**Technical procedure:**  
Perforate the print cumulatively with the tip of the pencil. After each section of perforation, place the paper in front of a white screen and photograph the light passing through the support. The sequence preserves fourteen selected states of the intervention.

**Script:** Not applicable.

---

## 3. Variables

**Controlled variables:**
- Perforation tool: pointed pencil.
- Luminous background: white screen.
- Cumulative order of the perforations.

**Uncontrolled / accidental variables:**
- Size, edge, and tearing of the paper around each perforation.
- Variations in position, reflection, and exposure during photographic documentation.

## 6. Crisis and formal-perceptual reading

**Main crisis:** Pending selection.

**The operation affects the visual grammar of the image: shape, outline, color, figure/ground relationship, legibility, recognition, and support:**  
The perforation turns the support into an active part of the image. The white light passes through the open points and replaces fragments of the figure with luminous emptiness; as they accumulate, the holes interrupt outlines, destabilize the figure/ground relationship, and cause the ball to lose continuity and recognition.
